I am writing a series of open textbooks. Each one assumes you know some math but
are new to the topic, and spends its time on why a method works rather than which
function to call. They are free to read, and the source is on GitHub.

## Starting with the t-test

Statistics. In progress.

When are you allowed to say two means differ? The t-test is the first tool most
people reach for and the one they misread most often. This book follows it from
the sampling distribution it rests on, through the assumptions it quietly makes,
to what a p-value does and does not tell you. Examples in Python.

## Algorithms Notebook

Computer science. Planned.

Data structures and algorithms with the code beside them. The aim is to make the
running time of a method feel inevitable once you see how it works, instead of
something to memorise from a table.

## Financial Data Analysis

Finance. Planned.

Returns, risk, and portfolios, using the tools from the statistics book on real
market data. Why financial returns break the assumptions that introductory
statistics takes for granted, and what to do about it.
